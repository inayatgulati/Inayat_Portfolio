Overview

The Pavilion

The Sculpture Pavilion is a conceptual architectural design that serves as an interactive exhibition space for sculptures — where the structure itself becomes part of the art.

Layered Panelling
Overlapping and intersecting panels create depth, rhythm, and a dynamic architectural language that changes as visitors move through the space.
Spatial Hierarchy
Two levels and dual staircases establish a clear hierarchy — upper overview versus ground-level immersion — giving visitors agency over their experience.
Light Interactions
Strategic voids in the panels allow controlled natural light penetration — generating shifting shadows throughout the day that transform how sculptures are perceived.

Project Goals

Design Intentions

Four principles guided every decision — from panel placement to visitor pathways.

Immersive multi-level exhibition space
Create a space where sculpture displays feel embedded in the architecture itself — not placed within a neutral container.
Integrate light and shadow dynamics
Use strategic panel angles and voids to generate a rhythmic interplay of light and shadow that shifts throughout the day, enhancing spatial engagement.
Design clear navigation pathways
Ensure fluid visitor movement through the space — dual staircases offer two distinct journeys through the same architectural experience.
Apply layered architecture principles
Balance aesthetics with functionality through overlapping, intersecting panels that create depth and rhythm without sacrificing structural clarity.
2
Challenges & Solutions

Three Problems,
Three Answers

Each structural and experiential challenge demanded a deliberate architectural response.

Challenge 01
Fluid navigation through open space
An open exhibition space risks disorienting visitors — no clear sense of direction or flow.
Solution: Defined pathways using layered panels and clear entry/exit points that guide movement without enclosing it.
Challenge 02
Structural integrity vs. openness
An open, airy design risks structural instability — especially across a two-story span.
Solution: Staggered and intersecting panels maintain form while enhancing visual openness — structure becomes the aesthetic.
Challenge 03
Maximising light without overwhelming
Too much direct light flattens the sculpture; too little creates a dim, uninviting atmosphere.
Solution: Voids placed in key panel areas allow controlled natural light — dappled, directional, and purposeful.

Design Process

From Sketch
to Structure

Four iterative phases took the pavilion from a rough two-story concept to a spatially nuanced, light-driven architectural experience.

Step 01 — Initial Sketch & Conceptualisation

The initial design envisioned a two-story structure with an inclined roof, emphasising symmetry and balance. Early explorations in Rhino revealed the potential for enhancing spatial generosity and refining movement pathways.

Initial sketch — two-story concept with inclined roof and symmetrical layout
Step 02 — Refining Structure & Layout

Using Rules 1 and 3 of layered architecture, panels were designed to overlap and intersect — creating depth and rhythm, with open voids for natural light penetration and a structured yet flexible navigation system.

Refined structure — overlapping panels, void placement, layered depth
Step 03 — Circulation & Interaction Design

Dual staircases offer two movement pathways — visitors can ascend from the right following a structured route through sculptures, or start at the upper level enjoying a bird's-eye view before descending.

Circulation diagram — dual staircase pathways and visitor flow routes
Step 04 — Light & Shadow Experimentation
Key Design Insight
Panels as light instruments
By positioning panels at strategic angles, the design creates a rhythmic interplay of light and shadow that dynamically shifts throughout the day — enhancing the sculptures' visual prominence while providing a constantly changing visitor experience.

Final Outcome

What Was Built

Three outcomes that define the pavilion's success as both an architectural and experiential project.

Visually engaging pavilion
Seamlessly blends art with architecture — the structure itself becomes part of the exhibition, not just a backdrop to it.
Multi-pathway exploration
Visitors engage with sculptures from different perspectives depending on their route — the same works feel different from above versus below.
Light-sensitive environment
Movement through the space changes how art is perceived — light shifts, shadows move, and the pavilion becomes a living frame for the sculptures within it.
6
Reflection

What I Learned

Three architectural insights that will carry into every spatial design project that follows.

Spatial hierarchy enhances function and form
Thoughtful organisation of space and materials doesn't just look good — it guides behaviour. The best architectural decisions serve both aesthetics and function without compromise.
Circulation is the design
A space that offers multiple paths rewards multiple visits. Perspective shifts improve visitor engagement and create dynamic experiences that can't be predicted in advance.
Light as an architectural element
Light isn't decoration — in this pavilion, it's structure. Controlling how it enters and moves through the space transforms everything from static to dynamic.
Structure becomes the aesthetic
The challenge of balancing structural integrity with openness taught me that constraints aren't limitations — staggered panels that solved an engineering problem became the pavilion's defining visual language.

The biggest realisation was that architecture is choreography. Every panel, void, and staircase is an instruction to the visitor's body — telling it where to look, pause, and move next.

— Inayat ✏️
